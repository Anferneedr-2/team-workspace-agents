# Workflow and journey mapping

Three different maps. Keep them separate, and confirm each one with the person before using it.

| Map | Answers | Who it is about |
|---|---|---|
| **Workflow** | How does the work get done today, step by step? | the team doing the work |
| **User journey** | How will a person ask the helper for things and use the answers? | the person who talks to the helper (the user) |
| **Customer journey** | What does the end person go through with the business, and where could the helper help or must stay out? | the client's customer, or Robi's own client |

The **user** talks to the helper. The **customer** is served by the business. They are often different people. Ask which is which.

## 1. Workflow map

Build it out loud, one step at a time. Then read it back as a numbered list.

| # | Step | Who | Starts when / input | What happens | Output | Where it gets stuck |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Also capture: **what starts it**, **what "done" looks like**, **decision points** ("if X then Y"), **exceptions** ("except when..."), **handoffs** (who gets it next), **tools and files used**, **how often** and **how much** (volume).

Questions to fill it: "What starts it?" "Then what?" "Who does that?" "What do they need to do it?" "What do they hand over?" "When does it go wrong?" "How often?"

## 2. User journey (the person who talks to the helper)

| Moment | What they are doing | What they need | What the helper should do | What they feel / worry about |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Capture: **who** the users are (role, skill level), **when** they reach for the helper, **where** they talk to it (app, chat, another helper), **how they phrase requests**, **what a good answer looks like** (length, format), **what annoys them**, **what must never surprise them** (for example, the helper sending something without asking).

## 3. Customer journey (the end person)

| Stage | What the customer wants | Where they meet the business | What frustrates them | Can the helper help here? | Must the helper stay out? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Typical stages to offer as a starting point, then adjust to their business: *finds you, asks questions, decides, onboards, receives the work, gives feedback, comes back or refers.* Use the person's own words for the stages if they have them.

Capture the **moments of truth** (the points that make or lose the customer) and the **hand-over points** where a human must stay in charge.

## 4. Tools and systems map

| Tool or system | Used in which step | Look only, or change? | Who owns the login | Must never be touched |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Each row becomes a **connection** in the brief (read-only first) or a reason the helper must ask first. If a tool has no safe read-only way in, say so.

## Turning the maps into helper requirements

| In the map | Becomes |
|---|---|
| A pain or slow step | the **job** the helper does (one job only) |
| A handoff | who it **reports to** or who it hands work to |
| A decision point | a **rule** in its instructions, or an **ask first** |
| An exception | a **never** or an **ask first** |
| The files and tools used | the **folder it works in**, the **built-in tools** it needs, and its **connections** |
| How often it happens | whether it needs a **timer** |
| The user's phrasing and format needs | its **tone and answer format** |
| A customer moment of truth | something it must **never do alone** |
| A touchpoint it should help with | a **first test task** |

## Checks before you use a map

- Did the person confirm each step in their own words?
- Is anything in the map something you assumed? If yes, ask or mark it Unknown.
- Is there more than one job in it? If so, it is probably more than one helper, or the first helper should take only one step.
- Is a helper even the right answer for each pain? A checklist, a template, or an existing helper may be better.

## Example (website agency, for tone only, not for reuse as facts)

Workflow: *client signs, gets the onboarding form, sends brand files, receives a site plan, approves it, build starts.* The slow step is chasing missing files. The user is the account manager. The customer is the client's own visitor, who meets the business on the website. A helper that chases missing files would touch only the onboarding step, ask before sending any message to the client, and never touch billing.
